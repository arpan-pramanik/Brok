# Goa: The Pearl of the Orient

Goa is India's smallest state by area, located on the western coast along the Arabian Sea. With a coastline stretching about 100 kilometers, it has been a sought-after destination for centuries. The state was a Portuguese colony for over 450 years before its liberation in 1961, making it one of the last European colonies in India.

## Geography

Goa covers an area of 3,702 square kilometers. It is bounded by Maharashtra to the north, Karnataka to the east and south, and the Arabian Sea to the west. The Western Ghats form the eastern boundary, rising to about 1,200 meters. The Mandovi and Zuari rivers are the two main rivers, both originating in Karnataka and flowing westward into the Arabian Sea. They divide Goa into three distinct regions.

The terrain ranges from flat coastal plains to the hilly Western Ghats. The coastal area features sandy beaches, laterite plateaus, and river estuaries. The interior is characterized by dense forest cover, waterfalls, and wildlife sanctuaries.

## Climate

Goa has a tropical monsoon climate. The monsoon season runs from June to September, bringing heavy rainfall averaging 2,500 to 3,000 mm annually. Summers from March to May are hot and humid with temperatures reaching 35 degrees Celsius. Winters from November to February are mild and dry with temperatures around 20 to 30 degrees Celsius, making it peak tourist season.

## Population and Demographics

As of the 2011 census, Goa has a population of approximately 1.46 million. The population density is about 394 people per square kilometer. Konkani is the official language, though Marathi, Hindi, English, and Portuguese are also widely spoken. Christianity and Hinduism are the two major religions, with a significant Muslim minority.
