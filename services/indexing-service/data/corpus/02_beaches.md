# Beaches of Goa

Goa's coastline is dotted with over 40 beaches, each with its own character. They range from busy tourist hubs to secluded stretches of sand.

## North Goa Beaches

Baga Beach is one of the most popular beaches in North Goa. Known for its nightlife, water sports, and shack culture, it draws large crowds especially during peak season from November to February. Tito's Lane nearby is the center of Goa's party scene.

Anjuna Beach was the birthplace of Goa's trance music scene in the 1960s and 70s when hippies discovered the area. The Wednesday flea market at Anjuna is famous for its eclectic mix of goods from clothing to jewelry. The rocky coastline here creates natural pools at low tide.

Calangute Beach, often called the Queen of Beaches, is the longest beach in North Goa. It is the most commercialized beach with numerous resorts, restaurants, and shops lining the shore. Water sports like parasailing, jet skiing, and banana boat rides are readily available.

Vagator Beach sits below the Chapora Fort ruins and is known for its dramatic red cliffs. It is quieter than Baga or Calangute and popular with backpackers. The iconic face of Shiva carved into the rock by an Italian sculptor is a landmark here.

Morjim Beach is known as Turtle Beach because Olive Ridley sea turtles nest here between October and March. It has become a haven for Russian tourists, with many Russian-run restaurants and shops. The beach is relatively peaceful compared to its southern neighbors.

Arambol Beach in the far north is the last bastion of Goa's hippie culture. It is known for its drum circles at sunset, the sweet water lake behind the beach, and paragliding from the nearby cliff. The community here is a mix of long-term travelers, yoga practitioners, and digital nomads.

## South Goa Beaches

Palolem Beach in Canacona is a crescent-shaped beach flanked by rocky headlands. It is one of the most picturesque beaches in Goa. Silent noise parties with wireless headphones are a unique attraction here. Kayaking to Butterfly Beach and Honeymoon Beach from Palolem is popular.

Colva Beach is the longest beach in South Goa, stretching about 2.4 kilometers. It has a more local feel compared to north Goa beaches. The Church of Our Lady of Mercy nearby hosts an annual festival in October.

Agonda Beach is consistently rated one of the best beaches in Asia. It is a long, wide stretch of sand with minimal development. Olive Ridley turtles also nest here. The beach is ideal for those seeking quiet and natural beauty.

Benaulim Beach is a quieter alternative to Colva, located about 2 kilometers south. It is known for its fishing village atmosphere and fresh seafood. The Goa Chitra Museum nearby showcases traditional Goan artifacts.
