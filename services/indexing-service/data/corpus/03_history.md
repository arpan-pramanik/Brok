# History of Goa

## Ancient Period

Goa's history stretches back to the 3rd century BC when it was part of the Mauryan Empire. The region was known as Gomanchala or Gopakapattana in ancient texts. The Kadamba dynasty ruled Goa from the 2nd century AD to 1312 AD, establishing their capital at Chandrapur (modern Chandor). They built the famous Tambdi Surla temple, the only surviving example of Kadamba architecture.

The Bhoja dynasty and the Chalukyas also controlled parts of Goa at various times. Between the 10th and 14th centuries, Goa changed hands multiple times between the Kadambas, Chalukyas, and the Rashtrakutas.

## Medieval Period

The Bahmani Sultanate conquered Goa in 1347. When the Bahmani Sultanate fragmented, Goa fell under the control of the Adil Shah dynasty of Bijapur in 1470. Yusuf Adil Shah made Goa Velha (Old Goa) his secondary capital.

The Vijayanagara Empire briefly held Goa before the Adil Shahis recaptured it. This period saw the construction of several mosques and the development of Goa Velha as a major port city.

## Portuguese Colonial Period

Afonso de Albuquerque captured Goa for Portugal on November 25, 1510, defeating the Adil Shah dynasty. This began over 450 years of Portuguese rule, the longest European colonial occupation in Asia.

The Portuguese established Goa as the capital of their eastern empire, known as Estado da India. Old Goa became a thriving city, often called the "Rome of the East," with magnificent churches and convents. At its peak in the 16th century, Old Goa's population rivaled that of Lisbon and London.

The Inquisition was established in Goa in 1560, targeting Hindu practices and forced conversions to Christianity. Many Hindu temples were destroyed during this period, and the local population suffered significant cultural and religious persecution. The Inquisition lasted until 1812.

The Portuguese built numerous churches, among them the Basilica of Bom Jesus (1605), which houses the remains of St. Francis Xavier. The Se Cathedral, Church of St. Francis of Assisi, and Church of St. Cajetan are other notable structures from this era.

## Liberation

India gained independence from Britain in 1947, but Goa remained under Portuguese control. Diplomatic efforts and the Satyagraha movement failed to convince Portugal to leave. On December 19, 1961, the Indian military launched Operation Vijay, liberating Goa after a 36-hour military action. Goa became a Union Territory and was granted full statehood on May 30, 1987.
