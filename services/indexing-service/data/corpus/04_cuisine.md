# Goan Cuisine

Goan food is a distinctive blend of Indian and Portuguese culinary traditions, shaped by 450 years of colonial influence and the state's coastal geography. Rice and fish form the staple diet.

## Seafood

Fish curry rice (xit kodi) is the quintessential Goan meal. The curry is made with coconut milk, kokum (a sour fruit), and a spice paste of dried red chilies, coriander, cumin, and turmeric. Kingfish (vison), pomfret, and mackerel are the most commonly used fish.

Prawn balchao is a spicy, tangy prawn pickle-curry that traces directly to Portuguese influence. The dish uses vinegar, a hallmark of Portuguese-Goan cooking, along with dried red chilies and a host of spices. It can be stored for days and actually improves in flavor over time.

Crab xec xec is a rich, dry crab preparation cooked with roasted coconut and spices. The name comes from the sound the spices make while being dry-roasted. It is typically served during special occasions and festivals.

Recheado masala is a fiery red spice paste used to stuff whole fish, particularly pomfret and mackerel. The paste contains Kashmiri chilies, vinegar, ginger, garlic, cumin, peppercorns, cloves, and cinnamon. The stuffed fish is then pan-fried until crisp.

## Meat Dishes

Vindaloo is perhaps the most famous Goan dish internationally, though it has been heavily modified outside Goa. The original Goan vindaloo comes from the Portuguese dish carne de vinha d'alhos (meat in garlic wine). In Goa, palm vinegar replaces wine, and local spices are added. Pork vindaloo is the traditional version.

Sorpotel is a Portuguese-origin dish made with pork offal and meat, cooked in a spicy, tangy gravy with vinegar and blood. It is traditionally prepared for Christmas and other celebrations. The dish is meant to be aged for several days, during which the flavors develop.

Chicken cafreal is a green spicy chicken dish with roots in Mozambican-Portuguese cooking. The marinade uses fresh green chilies, coriander, mint, ginger, garlic, and lime juice. The chicken is first marinated, then pan-fried.

## Sweets and Drinks

Bebinca is the queen of Goan desserts. This layered pudding is made from coconut milk, sugar, egg yolks, and flour, baked layer by layer. A traditional bebinca has seven layers and takes several hours to prepare. It is a must at Christmas celebrations.

Feni is Goa's signature spirit. Cashew feni is distilled from fermented cashew apple juice, while coconut feni (toddy) comes from the sap of coconut palms. The distillation process is traditional, using copper pot stills. Cashew feni received a Geographical Indication tag in 2009.

Port wine was historically produced in Goa during Portuguese rule. Today, several local wineries produce fruit wines from cashew, kokum, and other tropical fruits.
