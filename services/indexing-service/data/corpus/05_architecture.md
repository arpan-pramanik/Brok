# Architecture and Churches of Goa

## Old Goa (Velha Goa)

Old Goa, or Velha Goa, was the capital of Portuguese India from the 16th to 18th centuries. At its zenith, it was a grand city with a population exceeding 200,000. Disease epidemics, particularly cholera and malaria, led to its gradual abandonment in favor of Panjim (now Panaji) in the 18th century. Today it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, home to some of the finest examples of Baroque architecture in Asia.

## Basilica of Bom Jesus

The Basilica of Bom Jesus, completed in 1605, is the most celebrated church in Goa. It holds the mortal remains of St. Francis Xavier, the patron saint of Goa, in a silver casket within a mausoleum designed by Florentine sculptor Giovanni Battista Foggini. The church is the only church in Old Goa that is not plastered on the outside, exposing its laterite stone. The Exposition of St. Francis Xavier, where his body is displayed to the public, takes place once every ten years, drawing millions of pilgrims.

## Se Cathedral

The Se Cathedral, dedicated to St. Catherine of Alexandria, is the largest church in Asia. Construction began in 1562 and was completed in 1619. It originally had two towers, but one collapsed in 1776. The remaining tower houses a bell known as the Golden Bell, the largest in Goa and one of the best in the world. The interior features a main altar dedicated to St. Catherine and several chapels along the sides.

## Church of St. Francis of Assisi

This church dates to 1661 and combines Baroque and Corinthian styles. The interior walls are covered with frescoes depicting scenes from the life of St. Francis. The convent attached to the church now houses the Archaeological Museum of Goa, which displays a collection of portraits of Portuguese governors, Hindu sculptures, and colonial-era artifacts.

## Hindu Temples

The Portuguese destroyed most Hindu temples in the conquered territories. However, many deities were secretly moved to areas outside Portuguese control, primarily to Ponda taluka. The Shri Mangesh Temple in Priol is the largest and most visited Hindu temple in Goa, dedicated to Lord Shiva. The Shanta Durga Temple in Kavlem is another important shrine. The Tambdi Surla Mahadeva Temple, built by the Kadamba dynasty in the 12th century, is the oldest surviving temple in Goa. It was spared destruction due to its remote location in the Western Ghats.

## Portuguese Houses

Goa's residential architecture is a blend of Indian and Portuguese styles. Traditional Goan houses feature large verandas, shell-window grilles (nacre windows), internal courtyards, and tiled roofs. The Braganza House in Chandor is one of the finest examples, a 17th-century mansion divided between two branches of the Braganza family. Fontainhas in Panaji is a Latin Quarter with narrow streets lined with colorful Portuguese-era houses, many converted into heritage hotels and cafes.
