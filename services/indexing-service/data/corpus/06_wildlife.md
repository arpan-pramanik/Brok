# Wildlife and Nature in Goa

## Wildlife Sanctuaries

Bhagwan Mahavir Wildlife Sanctuary is the largest protected area in Goa, covering 240 square kilometers in the Western Ghats. It includes the Mollem National Park. The sanctuary is home to Bengal tigers, black panthers, sloth bears, Indian gaur, sambar deer, and barking deer. The Dudhsagar Falls, one of India's tallest waterfalls at 310 meters, is located within this sanctuary. The four-tiered waterfall is most spectacular during and just after the monsoon season.

Cotigao Wildlife Sanctuary in South Goa covers 86 square kilometers. It is known for its tall trees and dense forest canopy. Wildlife includes Indian pangolins, Malabar giant squirrels, flying squirrels, and various species of snakes. Treetop watchtowers provide views of animals visiting the watering holes at dawn.

Bondla Wildlife Sanctuary is the smallest wildlife sanctuary in Goa at 8 square kilometers. It functions as a combination of zoo and nature reserve. It is popular with families and has a deer park, botanical garden, and nature trails.

## Birds

Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary on Chorao Island in the Mandovi River is named after the renowned Indian ornithologist. It covers 1.8 square kilometers of mangrove habitat and is accessible only by ferry from Ribandar. The sanctuary hosts kingfishers, eagles, cormorants, kites, herons, and migratory species. The best time for birdwatching is October to March.

Dr. Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary is one of the important bird areas in India. Over 400 species of birds have been recorded in Goa, making it a significant destination for birders despite its small size.

## Marine Life

The waters off Goa support a diverse marine ecosystem. Dolphin watching trips depart from various beaches, with the Indo-Pacific humpback dolphin being the most commonly spotted species. Between October and March, whale sharks are occasionally sighted in deeper waters.

Olive Ridley sea turtles nest on Goa's beaches, particularly at Morjim and Galgibaga. Conservation programs run by local NGOs and the forest department protect nesting sites and monitor hatchling survival.

## Western Ghats

The Western Ghats portion of Goa is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the larger Western Ghats serial nomination. This region is one of the world's eight hottest hotspots of biological diversity. It receives heavy rainfall during the monsoon and supports evergreen and semi-evergreen forests. Endemic species include the Goan day gecko and several species of amphibians found nowhere else in the world.
