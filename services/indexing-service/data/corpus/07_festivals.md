# Festivals and Culture of Goa

## Carnival

The Goa Carnival is a four-day festival held in February or March, a tradition dating back to the 18th century Portuguese era. It begins on Fat Saturday and ends on Fat Tuesday before Lent. King Momo, a figure chosen to preside over the carnival, leads a colorful parade through Panaji with floats, dancers, and bands. Street parties, masquerade balls, and live music performances take place across the state. It is one of the few carnivals celebrated in India and draws tourists from around the world.

## Shigmo

Shigmo is the Goan version of Holi, celebrated over a fortnight in March. It is a spring festival marking the return of warriors from battle in historical times. The celebrations include street processions with elaborate floats depicting scenes from Hindu mythology. Traditional folk dances like Ghode Modni (a warrior dance with mock horses), Dhalo (performed by women), and Fugdi are performed during Shigmo.

## Feast of St. Francis Xavier

The Feast of St. Francis Xavier on December 3rd is the most important Christian festival in Goa. Thousands of pilgrims gather at the Basilica of Bom Jesus in Old Goa to venerate the saint's relics. The Exposition, held once every ten years, draws millions of visitors from around the world when the saint's body is displayed in a glass coffin.

## Sao Joao

The Feast of St. John the Baptist, celebrated on June 24th, is unique to Goa. Young men wearing crowns of fruits and leaves jump into wells, ponds, and rivers to celebrate the monsoon's arrival. The tradition symbolizes St. John leaping in his mother's womb when the Virgin Mary visited Elizabeth. The festivities include boat parades on the Siolim River and house-to-house singing.

## Music and Dance

Goa has a rich musical heritage blending Indian and Portuguese traditions. Mando is a traditional Goan musical form, a love song typically sung in Konkani with Portuguese-style mandolin accompaniment. It originated in the 18th century and is performed at social gatherings and weddings.

Goan trance music emerged in the 1960s and 70s when the hippie movement discovered Goa's beaches. The genre evolved into psytrance in the 1990s and continues to influence electronic music worldwide. Anjuna and Vagator were the original epicenters of the trance scene.

Fado, the melancholic Portuguese music genre, was once common in Goa. While it has largely faded, efforts are underway to preserve this musical heritage.
