# Economy and Tourism in Goa

## Economy Overview

Goa has one of the highest per capita incomes among Indian states. Mining, tourism, and agriculture are the three pillars of the economy. Iron ore and manganese mining in the Western Ghats was historically a major revenue source, though Supreme Court bans on illegal mining have significantly reduced this sector.

Fishing is a traditional livelihood. The state has a large fishing fleet, and fish is central to Goan diet and economy. Trawler fishing and traditional ramponkar (canoe) fishing coexist, though conflicts over fishing rights are common.

Agriculture is dominated by paddy cultivation, coconut plantations, cashew nut farming, and spice gardens. Cashew is a major cash crop, both for the nut and for feni production. The Old Goa spice plantation tours are popular with tourists.

IT and startup ecosystems have been growing in Goa, partly driven by the state's appeal to remote workers and digital nomads. Several co-working spaces have opened in recent years, particularly in North Goa.

## Tourism Statistics

Tourism contributes about 16 percent of Goa's GDP. The state receives approximately 8 million domestic tourists and 900,000 international tourists annually. The peak season runs from November to February when the weather is dry and mild.

Charter flights from Europe, particularly Russia, the UK, and Germany, are a significant source of international visitors. Dabolim Airport (now served by the new Manohar International Airport at Mopa) handles the tourist traffic.

## Types of Tourism

Beach tourism remains the largest draw. However, Goa has been diversifying into heritage tourism (Old Goa, Fontainhas, Chandor), eco-tourism (wildlife sanctuaries, spice plantations), adventure tourism (trekking in the Western Ghats, white water rafting on the Mhadei River), and medical tourism.

The casino industry is centered on floating casinos on the Mandovi River and land-based casinos in five-star hotels. Goa is one of the few Indian states where casinos are legal.

Backwater cruises on the Mandovi and Zuari rivers offer a quieter alternative to beach tourism. Sunset cruises with live music and drinks are popular evening activities.

## Challenges

Overtourism is a growing concern, particularly in North Goa. Infrastructure strain, waste management, water scarcity during peak season, and the impact on local communities are ongoing challenges. The state government has been exploring sustainable tourism models.

Real estate prices have surged due to tourism-driven demand, making housing increasingly unaffordable for locals. Land use conflicts between developers and traditional communities are common.
