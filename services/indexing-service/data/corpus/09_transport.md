# Transportation in Goa

## Getting to Goa

Goa is served by two airports. Dabolim Airport (GOI) in South Goa has been the primary airport for decades. The new Manohar International Airport (GOX) at Mopa in North Goa opened in 2023 and handles both domestic and international flights.

Madgaon (Margao) and Thivim are the two main railway stations. Madgaon is on the Konkan Railway connecting Mumbai to Mangalore. The Konkan Railway route passes through 93 tunnels and over 2,000 bridges, making it one of India's most scenic rail journeys. Direct trains connect Goa to Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Hyderabad, and other major cities.

Goa is accessible by road via National Highway 66 (formerly NH-17) along the coast and NH 748 connecting to Karnataka. State-run and private buses operate from Mumbai (12 hours), Bangalore (10 hours), and Pune (8 hours).

## Getting Around Goa

Renting a scooter or motorcycle is the most popular way to explore Goa. Rates range from 300 to 800 rupees per day depending on the vehicle. A valid driving license is required, though enforcement varies.

Auto-rickshaws are available but tend to overcharge tourists. Goa Miles and other app-based taxi services operate in the state, though traditional taxi operators have resisted ride-hailing apps.

Local buses operated by Kadamba Transport Corporation connect most towns and villages. They are the cheapest mode of transport but can be crowded and infrequent, especially in rural areas.

Ferry services cross the Mandovi and Zuari rivers at several points. The Panaji-Betim ferry and the Ribandar-Chorao ferry are commonly used by locals. These flat-bottomed ferries carry passengers, motorcycles, and even cars.

Houseboat rides on the Chapora and Mandovi rivers are available for tourists. They range from hour-long sunset cruises to overnight stays.

The Konkan Railway local trains connect Thivim, Karmali, Madgaon, and Canacona, useful for traveling between North and South Goa without road traffic.
