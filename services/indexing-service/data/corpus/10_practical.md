# Practical Information for Visiting Goa

## Best Time to Visit

The peak tourist season runs from November to February. Temperatures are pleasant, ranging from 20 to 32 degrees Celsius, with minimal rainfall. Accommodation and flight prices are highest during this period, especially around Christmas and New Year.

The shoulder season in October and March offers decent weather at lower prices. The monsoon season from June to September brings heavy rain and high humidity. Many beach shacks close during monsoon, and swimming can be dangerous due to strong currents. However, the landscape is at its greenest, waterfalls are in full flow, and prices drop significantly.

## Accommodation

Goa offers accommodation ranging from budget hostels at 500 rupees per night to luxury five-star resorts at 25,000 rupees and above. Beach huts and guesthouses in areas like Arambol, Palolem, and Agonda offer a middle ground.

Heritage hotels in converted Portuguese mansions are available in Fontainhas (Panaji), Chandor, and other old towns. Homestays with local families provide authentic Goan experiences.

During peak season, especially between December 20 and January 5, advance booking is essential as prices can triple and availability drops sharply.

## Food and Dining

Beach shacks are an integral part of the Goa experience. These seasonal structures set up on the beach during tourist season serve fresh seafood, Goan curries, and cold beer. Popular shacks include Curlies in Anjuna, Britto's in Baga, and Martin's Corner in Betalbatim.

Fine dining options exist in Panaji and at luxury hotels. Restaurants like Gunpowder, Bomra's, and A Reverie offer contemporary takes on Goan and Asian cuisine.

Local markets are the cheapest option for food. The Mapusa Friday market and the Panaji municipal market sell fresh produce, spices, and local snacks.

## Safety

Goa is generally safe for tourists. Common precautions include avoiding isolated beaches at night, not swimming after dark or while intoxicated, and being aware of undertows and rip currents. Petty theft from unattended belongings on beaches is the most common crime affecting tourists.

Drug laws in India are strict. Possession of even small quantities of controlled substances can lead to lengthy prison sentences. Despite the perception of Goa as a party destination, drug enforcement is active.

## Currency and Costs

Goa uses the Indian Rupee. ATMs are widely available in towns and tourist areas. Credit cards are accepted at hotels and larger restaurants, but cash is preferred at smaller establishments, beach shacks, and for transport.

A budget traveler can get by on 1,500 to 2,500 rupees per day. Mid-range travelers should expect to spend 4,000 to 8,000 rupees daily. Luxury travelers can spend 15,000 rupees and above per day.
